The Ultimate Workout for Upper Body Strength



The good news for you is that this upper body workout will help build serious upper body muscle and strength. The bad news for us is that it may be the last upper body workout we ever need to write. It's all here. We're done.


Of course, we're joking, variety isn't just the spice of life, it's also vital to keep your workout plan engaging. So, here's some more workouts to tuck away in your back pocket for when you're finished with this one.

  • Jason Fox's Upper-Body Pump and Sweat Session Ensures Stronger Lungs and Bigger Guns

Right, let's get to it, shall we?





How to Do The Ultimate Workout for Upper Body Strength



Created by Akash Vaghela, a senior PT at UP Fitness, this chest routine promises to build chunky slabs of muscle as well as significantly improving your strength.


'We'll be starting with the heavy work, and gradually move our way up the rep ranges as the workout progresses,' says Akash.


You've probably noticed that you are working through a fair few supersets. Supersets are a tried and tested method used by the world's best bodybuilders to increase your workout volume and increasing gains while saving time.


As building strength and size is the name of the game here, super-setting will benefit hypertrophy adaptations as you'll be hitting back-to-back muscle groups, pushing yourself closer to failure and therefore recruiting more muscle fibres.


Nail this workout twice a week and watch your upper body go from strength to strength. But, before we begin, one must warm up.





The Warm-Up



The purpose of this warm-up is to get the heart pumping, loosen muscles and prepare your central nervous system for the stress you are going to put your body through.



Rowing machine x 5mins:



Jumping on the rower will help elevate your heart-rate and increase joint mobility, loosening tight muscles in your legs, core and upper back.



Press-ups x 10:



These will help activate your chest, delts and triceps.



High plank x 30secs:



The plank is a great exercise to activate your core, which is essential for this workout.



Ready? Let's go.





Incline barbell bench press



Perform the next two moves as a superset


Sets: 4


Reps: 6


Rest: 90s


    How to:

  • Lie back on a bench set to an incline angle and lift a barbell to shoulder height, palms facing away from you.

  • Breathe out as you press up with both arms. Lock out your arms and squeeze your chest before returning slowly to the start position.


Top tip: Activate your glutes in order to maintain a solid foundation to work from.


CHIN-UP



Sets: 4


Reps: 6


Rest: 90s


    How to:

  • Grab the pull-up bar with your palms facing towards you and a grip that is narrower than shoulder width.

  • Pull yourself up until your head is above the bar. Slowly lower back to the start position.


Top tip: Do not sway. Maintain tension and form throughout. The stricter you are, the more competent at chin-ups you'll become.


UNDERHAND BENCH PRESS



Perform the next two moves as a superset


Sets: 3


Reps: 8


Rest: 1m15s


    HOW to:

  • Lie flat on an exercise bench holding a pair of dumbbells with an underhand grip.

  • Perform an underhand bench press by pressing the dumbbells upward away from the chest and then slowly lowering.

  • Forcefully press the dumbbells back up using your chest and triceps, following a slight arc back toward the starting position (over your upper chest/face).

ABDUCTION ROW



This might throw some of you off, but your next move is a pull-down movement.


Sets: 3


Reps: 8


Rest: 1m15s


    How to:

  • Start standing leaning forward with one hand resting on a surface and the other hand holding a heavy dumbbells.

  • Lean forward and drive into abduction by rowing the arm back and out with extra rotation at the shoulder. Pay attention to the range of movement in this exercise.

DUMBBELL CURL



Sets: 3


Reps: 8


Rest: 45s


    How to:

  • Keep your elbows tight to your sides and pull your shoulders back so that you're less tempted to use your deltoids.

  • Pick up the dumbbells and hold them at them top end of the dumbbell, rather than gripping them in the middle, as this will isolate those muscles a little bit more.

  • With your arms fully extended and in a neutral position (so your hands are facing each other) curl the dumbbells until your thumbs are near your shoulders.

  • Lower the dumbbell under control. That's one rep

PUSHUP PLUS



I'd like to focus on scapula control and strengthening, but I'm also looking for a little more chest volume, which is why I want you to perform some pushups, but not just any pushups.


Sets: 3


Reps: 20


Rest: 45s


    How to:

  • As far as proper form, go to the top of a push up, but continue pushing your upper body all the way through.

  • Aside from scapular strength, you'll also hit your serratus muscle, which is needed to perfect that complete six-pack look.

  • Perform three sets to failure.

Seated lateral raise



​​​Perform the next three moves as a superset


Sets: 3


Reps: 15


Rest: 10s


    How to:

  • Sit on a bench and hold a dumbbell in each hand by your side.

  • Raise both dumbbells to your side until they're shoulder height.

  • Lower under control and repeat.

BARBELL BENCH PRESS



This pushing exercise is a great upper body strength builder, which is one of our main mechanisms of driving growth through overload.


    How to:

  • Lie face up on an exercise bench with a wider than shoulder width grip on a loaded barbell. Keep shoulder blades tucked underneath you.

  • Lower the bar toward chest level and then push back up using the pecs.

  • Be sure to keep your ego in check. Avoid injury by aiming for this rep range and not your one-rep max.

  • Perform four sets of four to six repetitions, but do not take this to failure. You don't want to burn out on your first exercise.

Face pull



Sadly, you've reached your last movement of your second pull workout: the Face Pull.


    How to:

  • Attach rope handles to the top pulley on a cable machine. Kneel in front of it with one foot forward and flat on the ground.

  • Grab the handles and pull them towards your face, drawing your hands apart, taking care to keep your upper arms flat.

  • Pause, then reverse the movement.
